Why Your Welcome Flow Is Leaking Money (and How to Fix It in an Afternoon)

Joyal Johnson · Oct 12, 2026

Your welcome flow is probably the most-opened series of emails you'll ever send. Someone has just handed over their email address. They're curious, they're paying attention, and for a short window, your brand is genuinely on their mind.

And yet so many welcome flows I've looked at are just one email with a discount code, sent once, and then nothing until the next campaign. Or they're five emails that were built two years ago and haven't been touched since.

The good news? Most welcome flow problems are quick to fix. Here are the leaks I see most often, and what to do about each one.

Leak 1: The discount is buried

If your pop-up says "Get 10% off your first order," the very first thing someone should see in the welcome email is that code. Not a full-width lifestyle image, not "Welcome to the family," not your founder story. The code.

FixPut the offer in the subject line, the preview text and the top of the email. Make the code big, easy to copy, and add a button that takes people straight to your bestsellers. The brand story can come later. They asked for the discount, so give it to them first.

Leak 2: It's only one email

A single welcome email works for people who are ready to buy right now. But most people aren't. They signed up because they were interested, not because they'd already made up their mind.

FixBuild it out to three to five emails over roughly a week. Each one should do a different job: deliver the offer, introduce the brand, show social proof, answer objections, remind them the offer is ending. That way you're helping people who need a bit more time, not just the ones who were going to buy anyway.

Leak 3: Buyers keep getting "first order" emails

Someone signs up, uses the code, places an order. Then two days later they get "Don't forget your 10% off!" It's a small thing, but it makes the brand feel like it isn't paying attention.

FixAdd a flow filter in Klaviyo so anyone who has placed an order since starting the flow exits it. Then let your post-purchase flow take over. It's a two-minute change and it instantly makes the whole experience feel more thoughtful.

Leak 4: Every subscriber gets the same journey

Someone who signed up via a quiz about their skin type, someone who signed up from a blog post, and someone who signed up at checkout all have different levels of intent. Sending them the identical welcome series misses an easy opportunity.

FixYou don't need to overcomplicate it. Even one conditional split helps. A simple version: split by whether they've placed an order before. Someone who signed up at checkout after buying doesn't need a first-purchase discount; they need a warm welcome and a reason to come back. If your forms collect extra info (like a preference or product interest), use that to tailor at least one email.

Leak 5: The emails don't answer the "why not?"

People don't buy for reasons you might not think about. Is shipping free? How long does it take? What if it doesn't fit? Is it actually any good? If your welcome flow doesn't answer these, people quietly drift away.

FixDedicate one email in the series to objection handling. Pull the most common questions from your customer service inbox, reviews and FAQ page, and answer them directly. Pair it with real reviews. This email is rarely the flashiest one in the series, but it does a lot of quiet work.

Leak 6: No urgency at the end

If the discount never expires, there's no reason to use it today. Some people will sit on it forever.

FixGive the offer a sensible expiry and send a reminder email before it ends. Keep it honest. If you say it expires, let it expire. Fake urgency gets noticed quickly and chips away at trust.

Leak 7: It hasn't been touched in ages

Products change, branding changes, offers change. Welcome flows tend to get built once and forgotten, which means people might be getting emails featuring products you no longer sell or a tone that doesn't match your brand anymore.

FixPut a reminder in your calendar to review the welcome flow every quarter. Check that every product link works, every image is current, and every email still sounds like you.

How to know if your fixes are working

Before you change anything, note down the current numbers for each email in the flow: open rate (with a pinch of salt, since Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens), click rate, placed order rate and revenue per recipient. Then make your changes and compare after a few weeks.

The metric I'd care about most is the placed order rate across the whole flow, not just the first email. If more people are converting by email three or four, that's the sign your extra emails are actually earning their place.

The afternoon plan

If you've only got a few hours, do these in order:

  1. Move the offer to the top of email 1 and the subject line.
  2. Add the "placed order" exit filter.
  3. Add an objection-handling email with real reviews.
  4. Add an expiry reminder email.
  5. Check every link and product in the flow.

None of these are complicated. But together they turn a welcome flow from "one email and done" into something that actually turns new subscribers into first-time customers.
