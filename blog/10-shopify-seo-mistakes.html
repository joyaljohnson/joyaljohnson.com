10 Shopify SEO Mistakes That Show Up on Almost Every Store

Joyal Johnson · Aug 24, 2026

After digging through a lot of Shopify stores over time, the same handful of SEO mistakes turn up on almost every single one. Most of them are easy fixes that unlock real organic traffic once they're caught.

1. Duplicate content from product variants

When a product has multiple variants, size, color, material, Shopify often creates separate URLs using parameters like /products/classic-t-shirt?variant=12345. Google treats each as a separate page with near-identical content, which dilutes ranking power across all of them.

Fix: add canonical tags pointing every variant URL back to the main product URL without the parameter. Check the theme's product.liquid file to confirm canonical tags exclude variant parameters properly.

2. Missing or duplicate meta descriptions

Shopify auto-generates meta descriptions from product copy when none is written, often pulling incomplete text, and similar products frequently end up with near-identical descriptions as a result.

Fix: write a unique 150–160 character meta description for every product and collection page using the formula: what the product is + key benefit + call to action. Example: "Premium organic cotton t-shirt in 12 colors. Pre-shrunk, tagless, and built to last 100+ washes. Free shipping on orders over $50."

3. Thin collection page content

Collection pages target high-volume category keywords, but most contain zero actual content, just a product grid.

Fix: add 200–400 words of genuinely unique content above or below the grid, covering what the collection is, key features, how to choose between the products in it, and internal links to related collections or blog posts.

4. Unoptimized product images

Generic filenames like IMG_4521.jpg and missing alt text keep products out of Google Image Search and create real accessibility problems.

Fix: rename images descriptively before uploading (classic-cotton-tshirt-navy-front.jpg), and write descriptive alt text for every image including the brand, the product, and any variant detail. For a large catalog, a bulk SEO app can handle this at scale.

5. Broken internal link structure

Most stores have weak internal linking, products link only to collections, with no cross-linking between products or blog posts.

Fix: add "Related Products" and "Customers Also Bought" sections, link between related collection pages ("Women's Shoes" to "Women's Running Shoes"), include two or three product or collection links in every blog post, and make sure main navigation links to every important collection page.

6. Slow page speed

Google uses page speed as a ranking signal through Core Web Vitals, LCP, FID, CLS, and a slow store loses both visitors and search visibility. The usual culprits: too many apps injecting JavaScript, uncompressed hero images running 2MB or larger, heavy themes with unnecessary animation, stacked tracking scripts, and external font servers.

Fix: run Google PageSpeed Insights on the homepage, a collection page, and a product page, then work through the flagged opportunities in order of impact, image compression, removing unused apps, deferring non-critical JavaScript, and switching to system fonts where possible are usually the quickest wins.

7. No blog, or an abandoned one

Blogs are organic traffic engines that most stores underuse badly. A single eCommerce blog post ranking on page one can drive a few hundred visits a month on its own, ten ranked posts adds up to real, ongoing free traffic.

Fix: commit to two to four posts a month targeting keywords genuinely relevant to the products and customers. Buying guides, comparison posts, how-to content, listicles, and industry-trend pieces are all reliable formats. Aim for at least 1,500 words on competitive topics, with internal links back to products woven in naturally.

8. Missing structured data

Structured data tells Google what a page actually contains. Without it, results are plain links; with it, they can show star ratings, price, availability, and review counts, which measurably increases click-through rate.

Fix: add Product schema (name, description, price, currency, availability, brand, review rating), verify Review schema is outputting correctly via Google's Rich Results Test, and add Organization schema to the homepage with business name, logo, address, and social links.

9. Sitemap issues

Shopify auto-generates a sitemap, but it often includes pages that shouldn't be there, out-of-stock products, test pages, draft pages, paginated collections, and redundant URLs.

Fix: apply noindex tags to unwanted pages via theme code or an SEO app, set products to "hidden" where appropriate to prevent indexing, submit the sitemap in Google Search Console and monitor coverage regularly, and build a custom sitemap if full control is needed.

10. Ignoring Google Search Console

A large share of Shopify stores either never connect Search Console or haven't opened it in months, and it's a free tool that reveals ranking keywords, click data, and technical issues that nothing else surfaces as clearly.

Fix: check it monthly at minimum. The Performance report shows which keywords actually drive traffic, high impressions with low clicks usually means the title or description needs work. The Coverage report flags indexing errors and uncrawlable pages. Core Web Vitals surfaces Google-flagged speed issues. The Links report shows backlink sources and the most-linked pages on the site.

The audit checklist

  • Canonical tags correct on variant URLs
  • Unique meta descriptions on all product and collection pages
  • 200–400 words of content on collection pages
  • Alt text on all product images
  • Internal links between products, collections, and blog posts
  • Mobile PageSpeed score above 50, ideally 70+
  • An active blog with at least two posts a month
  • Product and review structured data verified
  • Sitemap submitted in Google Search Console
  • A monthly Search Console review actually on the calendar

Work through these in order and most stores will find real, recoverable traffic sitting in mistakes that take an afternoon, not a quarter, to fix.
