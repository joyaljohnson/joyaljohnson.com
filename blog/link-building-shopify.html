Link Building for Shopify Stores: What Actually Works

Joyal Johnson · Aug 1, 2026

Backlinks are still the number one ranking factor. Every algorithm update, every "SEO is dead" hot take, every new AI Overview rollout: none of it has changed that. What has changed is how hard eCommerce brands have to work to earn them.

Here's the problem specific to online stores: product pages have almost zero natural link appeal. Nobody wakes up and links to a product listing page just because they like it. Bloggers don't cite "Buy Now" buttons. Journalists don't reference add-to-cart flows. So if you're building a Shopify store and waiting for links to show up on their own, you'll be waiting a long time.

The fix isn't to abandon link building for eCommerce. It's to stop trying to get links directly to product pages and start building separate linkable assets that earn authority, then channel that authority back to your products through smart internal linking. That's the whole game.

Here are the six strategies that actually move the needle, plus the mistakes that quietly sink most link building efforts.

Six Link Building Strategies That Work for eCommerce

1. Ultimate Resource Guides

Comprehensive, genuinely useful guides on topics your customers care about are still one of the most reliable ways to earn links. Not a 600-word blog post, a real, exhaustive resource that becomes the reference point other people link to instead of writing their own version.

The key word is "comprehensive." If a blogger or journalist can find a faster or more complete answer somewhere else, they'll link to that instead. Aim to be the most useful page on the topic that exists, not just another entry in the search results.

2. Original Research & Data

Nobody links to a summary of someone else's findings. They link to the source. If you run a survey, analyze your own sales data, or publish a study nobody else has done, you become the citation, not the citer.

This doesn't need to be complicated. A simple customer survey with a clear headline stat ("62% of buyers said X") is often enough to get picked up, because journalists are constantly looking for a number they can attribute to somewhere.

3. PR and Press Coverage

Founders and team members who position themselves as expert sources, not as people promoting a product, get quoted, and quotes come with links. The pitch that works is "here's an interesting angle on a trend in this space," not "here's why our product is great." Journalists can smell the difference immediately, and only one of those gets a response.

4. Broken Link Building

This one is mechanical but effective: find resource pages in your niche, check them for dead links (404s), and reach out offering your content as a replacement. Because you're solving a real problem for the site owner (a broken link makes their page look neglected), the reply rate on this outreach is meaningfully higher than cold pitching.

5. Partnerships & Co-Marketing

Team up with brands that share your audience but aren't your competitors. A co-branded guide, a joint giveaway, a shared research piece: these create natural, mutual linking opportunities without anyone feeling like they're asking for a favor.

6. Unlinked Brand Mentions

The lowest-effort strategy on this list: find places online where your brand is already mentioned but not linked, and simply ask for the link. Set up a mention-tracking alert, check it monthly, and follow up. This alone can produce a steady trickle of easy wins most brands never bother collecting.

Mistakes That Quietly Kill Link Building Efforts

A few habits will undo everything above, no matter how good your content is:

  • Buying links. It works until it doesn't, and when it doesn't, it's a manual action that takes months to recover from.
  • Chasing irrelevant links. A backlink from a site with zero topical relevance to yours does very little for you, no matter how high its authority looks.
  • Over-optimizing anchor text. Exact-match anchors on every link is a pattern search engines are specifically built to catch.
  • Ignoring internal linking. All the external authority in the world doesn't help if it can't flow through your site to the pages that actually need it.
  • Fixating only on homepage authority. Product and collection pages need their own path to authority; they can't all rely on the homepage passing it down.

A Monthly System That Keeps This Moving

Link building falls apart when it's treated as a one-time project instead of an ongoing system. The brands that keep earning links month after month run something like this on repeat:

  1. Publish one linkable asset: a guide, a study, a piece of original data.
  2. Run targeted outreach to the sites most likely to actually care about it.
  3. Pitch one PR angle tied to a trend, not a product.
  4. Audit unlinked mentions from the past month and request the easy wins.

None of these steps is glamorous on its own. The compounding effect is what makes it worth doing: a resource guide published this month can still be earning links a year from now, and a founder quote in one article often leads to the next.

The Bottom Line

Product pages will probably never earn links directly, and that's fine. The stores that win at this treat link building as a separate content operation that feeds authority back into the store through internal linking, not as something they expect their product catalog to do on its own.

Pick two or three of the six strategies above, build a simple monthly rhythm around them, and give it time to compound.
